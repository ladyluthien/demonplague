Outside of the major towns in the Luna Valley, there are settlements that the maps of the world do not document. Goblinoids, garoul, and druidic/witch communities inhabit the wilds, each with their own lifestyles, traditions, and politics. **Unless you are one of these heritage (or a druid or witch class), you likely would not know the below information. Proceed at your own risk.**

### Goblinoid Communities
Goblinoid communities tend to be organized around a shared goal or living space and are relationships of convenience as much as they are anything else. Clans tend to range from 20-60 members, with childrearing done communally. Depending on how warfaring the clan is, children may live apart from the adults with a few parentally-inclined goblinoids raising them. Due to their history, goblinoids are often even more fiercely protective of their children than other communities, and it's not uncommon for even the smallest indication of threat to the clan's children to end in bloodshed.

Most goblinoid communities move periodically in the summers if they can before hunkering down for the winter, but a particularly defensible spot will be held for as long as it can be kept. Goblins in particular will paint the walls of a ruin to show that they lived there even after they have moved on. The art style is distinct and bold, featuring reds, oranges, and ochre colors as well as soot-black. 

### Garoul Clans
Garoul live in small migrant family units, although sometimes young garoul will form a short-lived clan together. These clans rarely interact except at major events such as weddings, funerals, or eclipses. A meeting of the full clans only occurs at an eclipse and is a grant event, full of discussions, revelry, and prayer. Conflicts between garoul or clans of garoul are usually held until the full moon and solved with a discussion - or a fight to submission or more rarely to the death, as agreed upon by the parties. 

Independence is important for young garoul. It is not uncommon for a young teen to go on multiple-day journeys away from their clan. 

More rarely, garoul who are humanoid-passing will attempt to assimilate into humanoid communities. Doing so almost universally means hiding their heritage and forgoing contact with other garoul. Garoul who live openly disdain these garoul, who they can often detect by scent. 

### Witches
A group of witches is usually called an argument, and as a result, most witches live alone or as the local witch for a community. However, there are rumors of a handful of covens in the wilds of the Luna Valley. These covens typically are formed of witches who all practice the same type of magic (white, black, or grey) and who have secretive goals or practices that bind them together. 

### Druids
Most druids are not, so to speak, "full-time" druids; reverence for the natural world does not always mean that one has to spend all one's time there. However, permanent druidic communities often form around key natural locations, such as the intersection of ley lines or the source-spring of rivers. These communities often treat the location with the reverence most others reserve for religion, although many druids would not consider it to be such. 

Less commonly, druids come together in pursuit of a common cause or goal, such as ending a famine or destroying a settlement that encroaches too recklessly on the wilds.
