There were four major settlements in the Luna Valley: Safeharbor on the coast, Sweetwater, Tomar's Crossing, and Hemlet. Of these, Tomar's Crossing is the only one thought to still be standing.

### Safeharbor
Safeharbor was the major metropolis (although still a small city by the standards of the Storm Coast) and boasted a population of about 60,000. The inhabitants were about 65% human, 8% elven, 8% orc, 6% dwarf, 5% halfling, 5% gnome, and 3% other races. It was overseen by a Council of Lords, a governing body of seven representatives, each from one of the noble families that resided there. Safeharbor - and indeed the whole valley - follows no queen or king, and no nation has felt the need to claim such a remote area.

## Sweetwater
Sweetwater was a medium-sized town of about a thousand, perched next to the only glacial lake in the valley. The glacier melts into the lake, creating a reasonably-sheltered area of bare land on the other side; as a result, Sweetwater is the main source of agriculture for the region. It was also the site of the yearly spring shearing and much of the tanning, weaving, and dying work. 

## Tomar's Crossing
Tomar's Crossing stands atop a hill that pokes out above the glacier. According to legend, it was named for an adventurer named Tomar who came to the area looking for a fabled [[Duladarin]] treasure trove under the glacier. According to the legend, Tomar quested for weeks, but never found a sign of the legendary fortune. As winter approached, he built a solid camp atop a hill. In spring, a small group of adventurers spotted his camp and approached, paying to stay in his well-equipped camp and learn from him about the area. A week later, another group of treasure hunters arrived, equally surprised to find Tomar alive and hearty, and this happenstance repeated several times that year as treasure seekers pursuing Tomar’s legend would crisscross the area questing and use the hill as a home base.

Eventually, Tomar declared the small hamlet of semi-permanent adventurers his, and he the leader. A few people who had given up or could not keep up with the hunt settled and planted crops or offered services to others in the area. Over the years the camp grew into a village known as Tomar’s Crossing. The treasure trove in question was never found, and most present-day residents care little for the story.

Today, Tomar's Crossing is a small frontier town of perhaps four hundred residents, mostly human. It is governed by a village council elected from amongst its residents.
## Hemlet
Hemlet was a small trapping town of a few hundred people. As the most remote settlement, it was also the wildest, with a population of humans, dwarves, orcs, and even the occasional goblinoid living in an uneasy truce. Many of the furs worn or exported in the valley are tanned here, in remote individual tanning operations.
