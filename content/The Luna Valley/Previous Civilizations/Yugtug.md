The Yugtugs were a clan of goblinoids that traced their ancestry to the mountains that surround the Luna Valley. Their legends hold that before they came down to the valley about two thousand years ago, the Luna Valley was a horrific place, full of monsters. The story, still shared amongst goblinoids today, is that these first Yugtugs fought mightily and bravely to carve out places of safety in the Luna Valley, and there the Yugtug civilization thrived for a time. (Real world parallel: you know people who are a little *too* into the wild west? That's how contemporary goblinoids might think about their ancestors.)

Little is left now of the Yugtug civilization; they built their forts from wood, unlike their predecessors, and as a result there are few remaining Yugtug ruins. What do remain are their clay tablets, upon which messages, records, and other things of note that needed to be preserved were recorded, and the legends passed down by generations of goblinoids. It is from these legends that the fall of the Yugtugs is recorded. 

It is said that the Yugtugs were destroyed from within by an evil cult known only now as the Cult of Chaos. The cult stirred distrust amongst the different Yugtug settlements, turning sister against brother and mother against child. It is also said that members of the cult had the ability to drive another mad with only their words. Yugtug civilization collapsed, although the occasional groups of goblinoids eked out an existence in the valley or in the mountains above.

What Yugtug artifacts that survive are usually bone or slate, cunningly worked to resemble beasts and monsters. Occasionally, more perishable materials such as wood and leather have survived as well, protected from the passage of time by the magic imbued within them. As is fitting with a culture that prided themselves on their martial strength, their weapons and armor are keen and well-made, and any beasts or monsters depicted are usually selected for their strength and ferocity (think bears, large fish, remorhazes, falcons, and the like). A combination of Viking and Diné, in terms of real world parallel.
