## Trade
The Luna Valley of the present day has little to offer outsiders. The biggest exports are furs, wool, and a rare red dye made from the small frostberries that grow during the summer. The furs are hunted and packaged inland, usually in [[Settlements|Hemlet or Sweetwater]]. The horned sheep native to the region are grown for both meat and wool, the latter of which is often processed by home spinners and weavers. On the coast, sailors fish and hunt seals and whales. Much of the food to sustain the Luna Valley during the long winter months is imported. 

The Luna Valley is large and dangerous enough that the transport of goods up- and down-river is an important task. Most goods travel by barge to the sea, where they are then sold to trading vessels. However, these barges are a prime target for thieves and bandits; guarding a barge can be a good source of income for a brand-new adventurer.

Because - aside from the coastal city of Safeharbor - the Luna Valley is so inhospitable and remote, many people who come here have a vested interest in being left alone and a willingness to defend that interest. 

## Food
Food is scarce in the Luna Valley, and its older inhabitants - primarily goblinoids and garoul - found creative ways to ensure that they could subsist here. Most humans, halflings, gnomes, dwarves, and elves currently living in the valley have settled here relatively recently and are willing to import staples rather than eat the food they find disgusting.

A traditional goblinoid (and/or prepper) diet is meat-heavy. Because of the short growing season, common vegetables and fruits include a variety of summer berries, often turned into jams or cordials; an allium somewhere between a leek and an onion that matures overwinter and is harvested in the spring from a fall planting; and a variety of chewy, earthy mosses. On the coast, this diet is supplemented with fish, seal, and seaweed; further inland, it is supplemented by caribou, sheep, and small mammals. Liver is a common delicacy, often eaten raw; so are blood sausages and puddings. Meat is typically kept frozen overwinter, so little other preservation is required; a deep winter dinner might be seared caribou chunks topped with sour jam, served with blood sausage, stewed onion and a portion of raw, jelly-like liver. 

Needless to say, for a city-born elf, such a diet is unpleasant at best. More recent transplants have attempted to overwinter more traditional crops such as potatoes and garlic and plant varieties of summer squash, radish, and cucumber that are able to withstand the short growing season. Some experiments with edible flowers have also yielded results, as has some honey cultivation. As a result, the import of whole grains, which are shipped whole and processed into flour in the valley, is a critical aspect of trade, and many residents of the Luna Valley rely on it.

## Dress
The cold temperatures of the Luna Valley make furs nearly essential. Furs are worn with the fur inward and the leather outward; to do otherwise would be a show of absurd luxury and is reserved for the nobles of Safeharbor. Bear, wolf (to the concern of garoul), rabbit, mink, and even lynx are trapped for fur, usually processed in small homestyle tanneries. Lamb and caribou skins are processed into leather in the tanneries on the outskirts of Sweetwater. Red-dyed leather is fairly common due to the berry dye that Sweetwater is known for, but leathers are also dyed black or green, or embossed in patterns.

Wool is worn year-round in a variety of weaves. Sweetwater was the primary fiber-manufacturing hub, where the long-coated sheep would be driven in the spring for shearing. The resulting wool would be carded into the thicker outer fibers and the thinner inner ones. The thicker fibers create a heavy and somewhat scratchy fabric in a tweed weave, high in lanolin and somewhat waterproof as a result. The thinner fibers are warmer and fluffier and single-woven into soft inner layers. Knitwear is reserved for socks, hats, and gloves; things that require stretch and come in tubes. Knitted undervests are also sometimes worn as a layer close to the skin, just over an underlying chemise.

Most residents of the Luna Valley, regardless of gender, will start their garments with a long loose woolen shirt in an undyed color. In the winter, most wear pants underneath and put a longer colorful dress or tunic over the shirt-and-pant combo. In the summer, it is feminine to forgo the pants in favor of longer, lighter dresses. 

Ornamentation comes in the form of embroidered or braided strips which are used as belts, ties, or edging. In the summer, it is also considered a feminine form of ornamentation to chew the bright red berries to tint one's lips; however, overdoing it can stain the teeth.
